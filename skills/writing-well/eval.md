# Writing Well eval

Run this on your output before returning it. Answer each check pass or fail. Fix any failure and re-run.

For detect requests, only the Detect section applies.

## Strunk

1. Is the voice active, with human subjects, wherever the sentence allows it?
2. Are statements in positive form?
3. Is the language definite, specific, and concrete — names, numbers, dates, mechanisms instead of abstractions?
4. Are needless words gone: empty qualifiers, throat-clearing, weak verb phrases ("made a decision," "has the ability to")?
5. Are related words kept together, and do emphatic words land at the end of the sentence?
6. Does each paragraph cover one topic and open with a topic sentence, unless the piece deliberately does otherwise?
7. Are co-ordinate ideas expressed in similar form, and is tense consistent in summaries?

## Words and patterns

1. Are banned words, filler phrases, empty adverbs, and inflated claims gone, unless quoted as examples?
2. Are binary contrasts, negative listings, rhetorical setups, and throat-clearing openers removed?
3. Are phantom contrasts gone — no "rather than merely," "not just," or "as opposed to" clause dismissing an alternative no reasonable reader would have believed?
4. Are faux-insight setups, editorial signposting, colon reveals, superficial `-ing` analysis, fake-strong verbs, synonym cycling, and dramatic fragments fixed?
5. Is importance puffery replaced with plain fact, and weasel attribution either sourced by name or flagged for the user?
6. Are significance tags gone — no trailing clause claiming a fact is meaningful or intentional ("and that's the point," "and that's deliberate," "and that's by design") in place of the mechanism that makes it so?
7. Are fake-profound kicker lines deleted rather than rewritten into better metaphors?
8. Are summary-recap endings cut, so the piece ends on a concrete point, takeaway, or next action?
9. Is formatting slop gone: emoji headings, decorative mid-sentence bold, bullets that should be prose, headers over tiny sections?
10. Is text after a colon in sentence case, unless grammar, a proper noun, a title, or code requires otherwise?
11. Are em dashes sparing — none in short copy, one or two in a longer draft, and only when they beat the alternatives?
12. Does the draft avoid robotic symmetry, counted scaffolding without a navigational purpose, repeated sentence shapes, and stacked punchy fragments?

## Editing (skip when drafting your own prose)

1. Does the edit preserve the user's point without adding claims, examples, stats, quotes, or opinions?
2. Does it preserve the writer's distinctive vocabulary, cadence, bluntness, humor, uncertainty, digressions, and level of polish?
3. Does it leave strong human sentences alone instead of rewriting them for consistency?
4. Is the amount of cutting proportional to the actual slop, with no compression that strips character?
5. Does the draft lead with what the reader needs, while keeping personal setup that adds context, tension, or character?
6. Are points front-loaded only where that improves clarity, without forcing every unit into the same structure?
7. Are genuinely tangled sentences fixed while clear spoken cadence, fragments, and changes of pace remain?
8. Does the edit keep useful edge, and keep the original structure unless the structure was hurting the piece?
9. Does the output include the full edited draft and a short **What changed** section?

## Detect

1. Does the response name each pattern found, quote the offending line, and give a short fix?
2. Does it avoid rewriting the draft, scoring it, or claiming AI authorship?
3. Does it judge each device by necessity and density instead of treating one matching phrase as proof of slop?

## Final read

1. Would the writer recognize this as their own voice?
2. Would it sound natural read aloud to a sharp colleague?
3. Did you check the output against this file directly, without needing a separate evaluator agent?
